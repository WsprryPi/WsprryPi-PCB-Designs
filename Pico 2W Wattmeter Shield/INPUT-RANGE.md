# Input range and calibration

The intended source is an unamplified Raspberry Pi GPIO clock generated by WsprryPi, with drive-strength settings of 2–16 mA. The frequency target is **136 kHz–148 MHz**, covering 2200 m through 2 m. Drive strength is not a calibrated current or RF-power setting; delivered power depends on the loaded waveform, frequency, filtering, and cabling. See the [Raspberry Pi GPIO electrical documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/raspberry-pi.html).

## Measurement range

| Location | Intended range |
| --- | --- |
| J1 input | Approximately −20 to +20 dBm, or 10 µW to 100 mW |
| Detector after the attenuator | Approximately −30 to +10 dBm, or 1 µW to 10 mW |

The **100 mW (+20 dBm) J1 ceiling is provisional**. Resistor-rating verification, thermal checks, and RF calibration are required before it is a guaranteed operating limit. The lower measurement limit and frequency response also require calibration.

The [ADL5904 datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adl5904.pdf) gives a nominal detector range of −30 to +15 dBm. With the onboard attenuator, that maps to approximately −20 to +25 dBm at J1, but the upper end exceeds the attenuator's resistor budget.

## Attenuator and power budget

R1 and R3 are 97.6 Ω; R2 is 71.5 Ω. With a nominal 50 Ω detector-side termination, the ideal pi network presents 50.481 Ω at its input and has **9.959 dB power loss**. These calculations treat C1/C2 as RF shorts and omit frequency-dependent parasitics.

| Quantity | At 54.45 mW input | At 100 mW input |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| R1 dissipation | 28.16 mW | 51.72 mW |
| R2 dissipation | 17.98 mW | 33.01 mW |
| R3 dissipation | 2.82 mW | 5.17 mW |
| Power delivered to nominal 50 Ω detector input | 5.50 mW | 10.09 mW |

At 100 mW input, calculated resistor dissipation is below half the saved 125 mW rating. Confirm the selected parts' manufacturer ratings and derating before relying on this budget.

An ideal 50% duty-cycle waveform switching between 0 and 3.3 V has 1.65 V RMS after DC removal, corresponding to **54.45 mW (+17.36 dBm)** into 50 Ω. This is a normal-waveform analysis bound for those rails and a resistive load. It does not imply that a GPIO maintains full voltage swing into 50 Ω or bound faults, overshoot, or resonant voltage step-up. Actual loaded GPIO power is a measurement result.

## Measurement conditions

The detector measures broadband RMS power. A raw GPIO clock contains harmonics, so total power differs from fundamental-only transmitter power. An ideal square wave has approximately 81% of its AC power in the fundamental; total AC power is about 0.91 dB higher. Real GPIO edges and the meter's frequency response affect that relationship.

Measure transmitter output after the intended band filter. Calibrate with a known RF source or reference meter at several power levels across the bands in use, including the LF and VHF endpoints, and compare Wi-Fi on/off conditions. Retain the filter, cabling, source model, clock configuration, and drive setting with the calibration data.

At 136 kHz, each ideal 470 nF coupling capacitor has about 2.49 Ω reactance. LF and VHF calibration cannot be assumed interchangeable. Source loading, supplied resistor ratings, thermal performance, and assembled RF response remain unverified.
