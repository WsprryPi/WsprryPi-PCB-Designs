# GPIO clock measurement range

The intended source is an **unamplified Raspberry Pi GPIO clock generated by
WsprryPi**, at drive-strength settings of 2–16 mA. The intended frequency coverage
is **2200 m through 2 m**, approximately **136 kHz to 148 MHz**. These are design
and calibration targets, not an already measured accuracy specification.

The operator does not need to know the output power before using the meter.
Drive-strength settings are not calibrated output-current or RF-power settings.
The output voltage under load, frequency, waveform, filter and cabling determine
the delivered power. See [Raspberry Pi GPIO electrical documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/raspberry-pi.html).

## Input design envelope

For an ideal 50% duty-cycle waveform switching between 0 and 3.3 V, AC coupling
removes the mean and leaves 1.65 V RMS. Into 50 ohms this is
1.65² / 50 = **54.45 mW (+17.36 dBm)**. For other duty cycles, the variance of a
waveform bounded by those same rails is no greater. This is a normal-waveform
analysis bound at a resistive load, not a claim that a GPIO can deliver full rail
swing into 50 ohms, nor a bound on faults, overshoot, or resonant voltage step-up.
Actual loaded GPIO power must be measured.

Use **100 mW (+20 dBm) at the SMA as the provisional maximum measurement input**
for design verification. This provides headroom over the ideal normal GPIO
example while keeping calculated resistor dissipation below half the saved
125 mW rating. Qualification and resistor rating verification remain required
before publishing this as a guaranteed operating limit. Do not replace this
with the approximately 242 mW resistor-rating boundary from the initial review.

For R1 = R3 = 97.6 ohms, R2 = 71.5 ohms and a 50-ohm detector-side termination,
the ideal pi network has 50.481-ohm input resistance and about 9.959 dB power loss:

| Quantity | At 54.45 mW input | At 100 mW verification ceiling |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| R1 dissipation | 28.16 mW | 51.72 mW |
| R2 dissipation | 17.98 mW | 33.01 mW |
| R3 dissipation | 2.82 mW | 5.17 mW |
| Power delivered to nominal 50-ohm detector input | 5.50 mW | 10.09 mW |

This calculation treats C1/C2 as RF shorts and ignores frequency-dependent
parasitics. The detector's nominal -30 to +15 dBm measurement range would map
approximately to -20 to +25 dBm before this attenuator, but resistor dissipation,
frequency response, and calibration limit the usable system range. The
provisional +20 dBm ceiling is deliberately below that nominal upper mapping.
No resistor or RF routing change is needed solely to meet the normal GPIO
analysis envelope. Source: [ADL5904 datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adl5904.pdf).

## What the meter measures

The detector responds to broadband RMS input power. A raw GPIO clock includes
harmonics; a measurement at its nominal frequency is not automatically a
measurement of fundamental-only transmitter power. An ideal square wave has
8/pi², approximately 81%, of its AC power in the fundamental: its total AC power
is about 0.91 dB greater than its fundamental power. Real GPIO edges and the
meter's frequency response change that relationship.

For transmitter output power, measure after the intended band filter and retain
that filter, cabling, source model, clock configuration and drive setting in the
measurement record. Calibration should cover the lowest LF band, every band
actually used, and the highest VHF frequency, at several levels up to the
provisional ceiling. Use a known RF source/reference meter; include Wi-Fi on/off
comparison. At 136 kHz, each ideal 470 nF coupling capacitor has about 2.49 ohms
reactance, so do not assume the LF and VHF calibration are interchangeable.

No measurement or hardware test was performed for this document. Manufacturer
rating/derating verification for the selected attenuator resistors, assembled
RF calibration, and source loading remain open physical checks.
